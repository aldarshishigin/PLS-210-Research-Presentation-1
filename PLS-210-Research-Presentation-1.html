Sakha students’ awareness of traditions: Yakutia vs. Kazakhstan, in their own words

“How do Sakha students’ awareness of traditions differ comparing students who study in Yakutia and students who study in Kazakhstan in their own words.”

A comparative, interpretivist study of ethnic and civic identity.

Akira Handa (pseudonym) · PLS 210, Fall 2026

Research question

How does Sakha students’ awareness of traditions differ — Yakutia vs. Kazakhstan, in their own words?

YakutiaStudying at home — a subnational republic within Russia
KazakhstanStudying in a sovereign Turkic state, independent since 1991
  • Awareness — what students say they notice and know
  • Traditions — left undefined; students set the category
  • Kazakhstan — a specific comparison, not generic “abroad”

Epistemological positioning

Interpretivist: students’ own meanings are the evidence.

  • Evidence = what students say — not measured against an outside yardstick
  • “Traditions” left undefined — students set the category, not me
  • No statistical generalization — a detailed account of two settings
  • One provisional expectation, not a hypothesis — a concession to the course’s deductive lean

Contribution

Compares two settings. Names the difference. Lets students speak.

Empirical

  • Sakha students’ awareness of traditions, in their own words
  • Yakutia vs. Kazakhstan

Conceptual

  • Civic and ethnic identity kept apart
  • Kazakhstan: sovereign since 1991 — Yakutia: subnational republic
  • Both ethnicities linked as Turkic

Methodological

  • Starts from students’ own categories — no presupposed cultural loss
  • Kazakhstan ≠ generic “abroad”
  • Fills a gap: existing work is quantitative or set in Western contexts

Literature review

Sakha post-Soviet and ethnic identity

Post-Soviet Sakha identity: cultural restoration + integration into Russian federalism.

Giuliano (2011)

Identity shaped by political & economic grievances.

Cruikshank & Argounova (2000)

Identity restored through memory & oral history.

Yakutia’s education is mostly Russian-medium — students navigate both a dominant framework and their own cultural memory. Interpretivism captures their first-person view of that dichotomy.

Literature review

Minority students studying abroad

Sude et al. (2020)

Abroad reinforced minority or national identity — or neither.

Sobkowiak (2019)

Opposite angle: majority-ethnicity Polish students.

This study

Sakha students who went abroad right after graduation vs. those who stayed.

Literature review

Kazakhstan’s national identity context

YakutiaSubnational republic within the Russian Federation
KazakhstanSovereign statehood since 1991

Two contexts, not equivalent.

  • Gov’t balances civic and ethnic identity — but perceived largely through an ethnic lens (Sadvokassova et al., 2025)
  • Kazakhstan: ethnic restoration backed by civic identity
  • Contrast: Yakutia’s subnational status

Literature review

Where the debates leave a gap

  • Ethnic restoration, memory politics, post-Soviet context, student mobility, civic vs. ethnic identity — studied separately
  • This study: subnational-republic students choosing a sovereign Turkic state, vs. those who stayed

References · Giuliano (2011) · Cruikshank & Argounova (2000) · Sude et al. (2020) · Sobkowiak (2019) · Sadvokassova, Burkhanov & Sharipova (2025)
